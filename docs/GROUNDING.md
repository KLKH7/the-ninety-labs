# Grounding: where every rule comes from

The match engine and the validator both encode football. Neither is built from memory: every
rule, limit and realism band cites a source, and this file is the register. The engine uses it
to *make* football; the validator uses it, through independently written code, to *check* it
([architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md#synthetic-data-pipeline)).

**Status: draft for review.** Every Law row was checked on 2026-10-13 against the Laws as
published on [theifab.com](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-field-of-play/) (the site
lists the 2026/27 edition first). Values still marked *verify* come from general knowledge and
must be checked against the cited source before their check is built.

## Sources, by reliability

| Tier | Source | What it gives us | How we use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **IFAB Laws of the Game** (current edition, [theifab.com](https://www.theifab.com)) | the rules, definitively | rules (hard checks), dimensions |
| 2 | **Peer-reviewed sports science**, preferring elite or Premier League samples, larger samples, recent, open access | physical limits, with ranges | physics limits, with generous margins |
| 3 | **Official competition reports** (league and UEFA technical reports) | season averages | realism bands (warnings) |
| 4 | **Industry standards and preprints**, e.g. the [Common Data Format](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15820) | how match data is structured and synchronised | structure of our data, never numbers |
| not used | stats websites, AI-generated summaries | | methods unclear, terms often forbid scraping |
| ask first | real match datasets, even free open ones | | rule 4.7 requires synthetic data; ask the organisers before any use, even offline |

## What we may and may not do with each

| Source | Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|---|
| IFAB Laws | read the official PDF; implement each rule as code; cite the Law and section; summarise in our own words | committing or reproducing the text: IFAB's notice requires its authorisation to reproduce or translate the Laws, in whole or in part |
| Sports science papers | cite figures with a link; prefer open access | copying text, tables or figures |
| Competition reports | cite a handful of published averages | scraping, bulk copying |
| CDF, kloppy | read; use kloppy's code (BSD-3) if it earns its place | |

Rules and facts are implemented, never copied. This is our reading of the licences and the
hackathon rules, not legal advice; when unsure, ask the organisers.

## How a source becomes a check

1. **Read** the primary source (for a rule, the Law in the current IFAB edition).
2. **Register** it below: one row, in our own words, with the citation.
3. **Make it measurable.** "The whole ball crosses the line" becomes "the ball's centre is past
   the line by more than its radius (0.11 m)".
4. **Plant a defect** in a test clip and require the check to fail on it.
5. **Build the check** in `ninety/validate.py`. Shared *facts* (pitch size, ball radius) may live
   in one constants module both sides import; the *logic* is written separately.
6. **Run it on the engine.** A failure is the engine's to-do list; the check stays forever.

### Decisions: the player's own estimates for choosing

These are estimates a player uses to choose, from his own picture of the pitch; the outcome of
the chosen action is decided by physics and execution error, so an estimate can be wrong.

| ID | Rule | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | each option scores expected value: the chance it works times the value of what follows, minus the chance it fails times the cost of losing the ball where it would be lost (along the lane or where it was aimed, the worse). A pass's chance (`pass_confidence`) is the passer running, on his own picture of the pitch, the race the engine runs for a free ball: the first team-mate who can get to the ball's path takes it, by the chase's own reach test (`body.can_get_to`, shared with `engine.can_reach`) with an average body (P2, P9, P10) after an average reaction (P12; the simple one for the team-mate it is meant for, who expects it), along the ball's own flight (`ball.predict` until it rests, bounded by `ball.FLIGHT_BOUND_S` as `engine.ball_destination` is; the chase looks 6 s ahead but re-plans from wherever the ball is, so over a pass it follows it as far; checked every second frame as the chase does). It fails if (a) an opponent's body is on its path before he can react (body contact needs no reaction: `engine.contact`; how sure the passer is of where the opponent stands is his perception error, P13), (b) an opponent can get there before that team-mate (each raced against the take, with an average reaction; a keeper in his box reaches with his hands, P11, at a ball in flight or at rest; one who cannot quite get to the path is raced by how late he would be, the soonest he could be at a point of it if the ball waited there, as the engine's chase times a chaser who cannot make it, so the estimate moves smoothly as he nears the lane; the price of that continuity is some pessimism, since a late opponent is credited with the time the ball passed a point, not with the later time he gets there and finds it gone; *verify*), (c) the team-mate's first touch cannot kill the ball's speed relative to him (A4: his own `absorb_mps`, and the same relative speed the engine judges, `body.relative_speed`; reach and Law 12 by the engine's own `offball.reach_for` and `offball.may_handle`, Law 9 by `decisions.out_of_play`, which `engine.went_out` also uses), or (d) it leaves the pitch first (Law 9). The whole race is averaged over the strikes his own kick error allows (A3: the same `Profile.strike_error` the kick is struck with, for his skill, the speed, the nearest opponent he sees, his fatigue and the foot), by a deterministic quadrature: the centre and plus and minus root-3 SDs along each error, weights 1/6, which keeps each error's first four moments (three-point Gauss-Hermite). If he must first turn to face within 60° of it (P16), it is estimated struck at that angle after that turn, with team-mates and opponents moved on by their velocities for the turn's duration. The pass is struck as it was estimated (ground pass arriving at 6, 10 or 14 m/s, lofted at 30°), then execution error is added. *Design*: the 0.25 s that softens the race (LANE_MARGIN) and the 2 m/s that softens the first touch (CONTROL_MARGIN), the paces and the loft; an opponent 2 s (8 margins) later than the take, or whose body passes more than 5 SDs of the perception error outside contact distance, is not counted (each worth under 0.04%); the passer judges a strike's launch to 0.05 m/s. *Verify*: the chase's reach test (`body.can_get_to`, the engine's) treats a player within reach but running away as either staying in reach while he brakes or having to brake and come back, a step that still moves the estimate by up to about 0.03 per millimetre at that edge; a failed first touch counts as lost, though the ball may still come back to his side; a team-mate's body in the way is ignored; every opponent is assumed to react in the average 0.365 s; the team-mate's velocity at the take is the one the passer saw; a team-mate not seen for 2 s still counts 0.8 | expected-value and pass-lane models in performance-analysis research; the race, contact, first-touch and kick-error rules are the engine's own (rows P2, P9 to P13, A3, A4); calibration against the engine in [docs/eval/pass-calibration.md](eval/pass-calibration.md) | ✅ |
